# Free Triage Call — Script

20 minutes. Goal: locate the mess, establish the no-lose frame, book the report.
Not a sales call — a sorting call. If there's no problem, say so and let them go.

---

## Opening (first 60 seconds — say almost exactly this)

> "Thanks for booking this. Here's how the next 20 minutes work, so there are no
> surprises: you tell me what happened, in your own words, and I won't interrupt.
> Then I'll ask a few specific questions. Then I'll tell you one of three things:
> what you're describing sounds like a real problem and here's how I'd approach it;
> it sounds like X, which you can fix yourself in an afternoon, and I'll tell you
> how; or I'm not sure yet, and here's what would settle it. Fair?"

Then: **stop talking.** Their uninterrupted story is the intake (INTAKE.md §1).
Write down their exact words for the mess — you'll reuse them in the report's
executive summary. People buy when the report opens with their own sentence.

## Key questions (pick per situation — never all)

1. "Which tools were involved, and do you still have the chat histories?"
   *(If yes: "Good — don't clean anything up. Those transcripts are the evidence.")*
2. "What's the worst realistic outcome if nothing changes this month?"
3. "Has anything like this happened before, or is this the first time?"
4. "Is there a date this has to be resolved by?"

## The money moment (minutes ~15–18)

Restate their mess in one sentence, calmly, using their words:

> "So: your AI assistant told you it rebuilt the invoicing system, and when you went
> to use it, the files weren't there. That's what we're solving."

Then the no-lose frame — this is the hinge of the entire call:

> "Here's the thing that makes this an easy decision. Your tools keep written records
> of everything they claimed to do, and every one of those claims can be checked
> against your actual files in minutes. There are only two possible results.
> If the claims check out — you now *know* your work is real, which is worth more
> than what the report costs. If they don't check out — we just found the problem
> before your customers did. **There's no result where checking was a mistake.**
> The only losing move is hoping."

Then the offer, one sentence, fixed price:

> "The Damage Triage Report is $250, done in 48 hours. You get the findings, the
> prioritized fix list, and the raw evidence with the exact commands to verify
> everything yourself — because if I'm wrong about anything, you should be able
> to prove it."

Then **silence.** First one to talk loses nothing, but let them sit with it.

## The three objections

### 1. "Can't I just check this myself / ask the AI?"

> "Yes — and here's the guide for doing exactly that, free." *(send the re-verify
> commands from a sample report)* "Two things: first, most people who could have
> checked themselves are calling me because they tried and the tool talked them out
> of what they saw. Second, the check isn't the hard part — knowing which phantoms
> are damage and which are noise, and what to fix first, is. You're not paying for
> the search. You're paying for the judgment."

### 2. "$250 seems like a lot."

> "Compared to what? If the report finds nothing wrong, you paid $250 to stop
> worrying and sleep — and that's the *bad* outcome for me, honestly, because you
> won't need the bigger services. If it finds real damage, what's the price of
> finding it three weeks later, after it's in production? The report is the cheap
> branch of the tree either way. And it's fixed — if it takes me twenty hours,
> that's my problem, not your invoice."

### 3. "I already know what the problem is / my cousin looked at it."

> "Maybe you're right — and if your diagnosis is right, the report will confirm it
> in writing, which is worth having before you spend money on the fix. But may I
> ask what the assistant *told* you the problem was?" *(listen)* "That's the trap
> with AI-caused messes: the same tool that made the mess narrates the diagnosis,
> and it narrates it confidently. Its record of what it did is sitting right there.
> We check the record, not the story. One hour of evidence beats ten hours of
> theories — mine included."

## The three exits (no problem / wrong fit / scam)

- **No real mess:** "Honestly? This sounds like normal tool flakiness. Here's what
  I'd do instead, and it's free: [exact steps]. Keep my page for the day it isn't
  flakiness." *(This exit builds the referral engine. Never fake a problem.)*
- **Wrong fit** (needs development work, IT helpdesk, therapy): name it, refer out,
  keep the door open.
- **They're being scammed:** "What you're describing has a name, and it's not a
  technical problem — it's a crime. Here's the exact authority to report it to, and
  what to preserve first. I'm the wrong hire for that, and I'd rather tell you now."

## After every call, regardless of outcome

Log it: date, one-line situation, outcome, referral source. Ten logged calls will
tell you which service actually sells, which objection wins, and what to put on the
landing page next. The calls are the market research.
