# Mess-to-Map Report

**Status:** ACTIVE — entry product. Cheapest, fastest, zero tooling.
**Price:** $100–150 · **Turnaround:** 24–48h · **Instrument:** none required —
manual reading and structuring. This is the volume/entry tier that feeds
everything else.

## Offer (one sentence)

> "Send me your mess — notes, screenshots, transcripts, exports, a folder of
> recovered files — and I'll send back a clear map of what you have, what
> matters, and what to do first."

## Why it sells

The audience this practice serves is, almost by definition, buried: recovery
folders from a Powerwash, chat exports from three different AI tools, a
Downloads directory with 40 screenshots of an error, a notes file that's 200
unordered lines. They can't see the shape of what they have. The humiliation
tax on asking for help is low because they're sending *stuff*, not admitting
failure — "make sense of this" is easier to ask for than "I got scammed" or
"my codebase is a disaster."

## Process

1. **Intake** (light version of INTAKE.md): what is this mess, what do they
   hope is in it, what's the decision it's supposed to inform
2. **Inventory pass**: every item cataloged — type, date, apparent relevance
3. **Cluster**: items grouped into threads (the "this + this + this = one story"
   step that's nearly impossible to do on your own material)
4. **Map**: one diagram/table of the threads and how they connect
5. **Next actions**: 3–5 concrete steps, each traceable to specific items

## Deliverable (2 pages max)

1. Situation summary (their words, cleaned up)
2. The map (threads, key items, dead ends called out honestly)
3. What's missing (gaps in the record — "you have no export from before X")
4. Next actions (numbered, each with the item that justifies it)

## Rules

- If the mess reveals a bigger problem (scam, legal exposure, codebase
  collapse), the report says so plainly and quotes the relevant service —
  this is the upsell path, never manufactured, only surfaced
- 24h turnaround is the product. If it would take longer, say so and requote
- No item in their mess is ever mocked. Everyone's mess looks stupid from the
  outside; the report treats it as evidence, not embarrassments
